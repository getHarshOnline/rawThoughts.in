# Read the findings

A creator announces that daily clips will replace weekly long-form explainers. A RawFish report suggests that regular viewers may read the change as abandonment. The announcement tells you what will stop; finding out how viewers interpret it requires another kind of evidence. Reading the report means following that difference through its claims.

<a id="start-with-the-run-that-produced-the-report"></a>

## Start with the run that produced the report

In Findings, **Read** opens the report, **Ask** lets you question it and **Analysis** shows settings, lineage and comparisons. Keep the scenario, model and configuration alongside the claims you examine.

The current Creator prototype asks a model to write this report after recording a completion marker. It runs no agent conversation, and its report prompt uses neither the cohort statistical settings nor the evidence catalog. Treat its proposed audience reactions as questions to investigate, including any labelled “simulated observation.” The [reference-prototype explanation](https://rawthoughts.in/rawfish/use-the-local-prototype/) follows the complete announcement through that path.

<a id="ask-what-supports-each-claim"></a>

## Ask what supports each claim

The report model uses five labels. In the planned rebuild, each claim would also connect to the appropriate source or simulated event. An illustrative parking-policy rehearsal shows the different kinds of support:

| Label | Example | What would support it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Grounded in source | The policy assigns enforcement to a contractor | The passage in the supplied policy |
| Simulated observation | A traders' cohort objects to losing customer parking | An action or interview recorded in the simulated run |
| Inference | The objections appear to be coordinated opposition | The observations and reasoning behind that interpretation |
| Assumption | The traders' association will not endorse the policy | The stated premise, who supplied it and what changes if it is wrong |
| Needs validation | A free pilot might change real traders' response | Investigation with the people affected |

A label tells you what to look for; it cannot supply missing evidence. Even with recorded simulated events, claims about equity or workplace adoption, morale and dissent still need real-world validation. Generated personas are not a sample of real people.

<a id="use-ask-to-test-the-explanation"></a>

## Use Ask to test the explanation

Return to the [creator announcement](https://rawthoughts.in/rawfish/use-the-local-prototype/#continue-through-the-six-cockpit-steps) and ask:

> Which concern comes directly from the announcement, and what recorded action supports the claimed audience reaction?

Check the answer against the report and available data. You can verify that new explainers would stop by reading the announcement. There is no recorded Creator conversation to support the proposed feeling of abandonment, so that question goes to actual viewers.

**Caution**

**Keep quotations with their source.** A generated quotation must not be presented as testimony.

<a id="compare-the-change-you-actually-made"></a>

## Compare the change you actually made

Suppose the creator revises the announcement to keep one long-form episode each month. Comparing that proposal with the original requires two separate runs, their other inputs aligned and both valid reports retained. Read them side by side to see whether they raise different questions about the archive, the clips or the remaining episode. Model wording can vary even when the premise stays the same; neither agreement nor disagreement tells you how many real people share a concern.

The prototype's [settings comparison](https://rawthoughts.in/rawfish/use-the-local-prototype/#compare-settings-before-comparing-reactions) does a different job: it contrasts saved configuration with its parent snapshot. It helps you check what changed in the settings, but does not compare audience reactions or show an effect from changing the seed.

<a id="decide-what-to-investigate-next"></a>

## Decide what to investigate next

Turn a concern into a concrete action: clarify the announcement, check an operational constraint, interview affected people or try a reversible pilot. Keep the unanswered question with it. “Would a monthly long-form episode address the concern?” gives the creator something to investigate. The report cannot settle that by declaring that the audience rejects daily clips.

The [rehearsal inquiry](https://rawthoughts.in/collections/systems/rehearsal-and-evidence/) develops the broader questions behind imagined audiences and changed premises. [Rehearse a decision](https://rawthoughts.in/rawfish/rehearse-a-decision/) explains the workflow linking claims to their evidence.
