# RawFish

Before the other products carry out a decision, RawFish helps you rehearse how it could go wrong. Bring a proposal, the people it could affect and a question you can still act on.

<a id="explore-how-a-decision-could-go-wrong"></a>

## Explore how a decision could go wrong

Among [the products](https://rawthoughts.in/products/), RawFish rehearses a direction before anyone commits to it. It sits between the records [Weave](https://rawthoughts.in/weave/) keeps and the act that follows a decision, and [Pale](https://rawthoughts.in/pale/) could coordinate a rehearsal with its follow-up investigation. Rehearsing decides nothing; the team still decides what to change after examining the findings.

Follow a creator announcement, workplace change or civic decision through simulated interactions. If a report raises an unexpected concern, trace it to the assumptions or events that produced it and decide what to ask the people affected. [See how a rehearsal works](https://rawthoughts.in/rawfish/rehearse-a-decision/).

## From a decision to a better question

Build a setting, follow the simulated interactions and investigate what produced an unexpected reaction. Use what you find to improve the proposal or decide what to test with the people affected.

1. 
   
   <a id="frame-the-world"></a>
   
   ### Frame the world
   
   Bring the decision, source text and what would change your mind. Inspect the graph, affected cohorts and their activity or influence assumptions before accepting the setting.

2. 
   
   <a id="rehearse-the-interactions"></a>
   
   ### Rehearse the interactions
   
   Choose a local model or external provider and inspect readiness, scale and cost before running. Follow the frozen configuration through simulated rounds on the timeline.

3. 
   
   <a id="inspect-a-finding"></a>
   
   ### Inspect a finding
   
   Read the report, ask about a claim or open its supporting data. Follow an unexpected reaction back to an interaction, source statement or assumption.

4. 
   
   <a id="compare-and-investigate"></a>
   
   ### Compare and investigate
   
   Give a second version of the proposal its own scenario and report, keep the other settings aligned and read the two reports side by side. Turn the differences into a revised alternative, a conversation with the people affected or an operational test.

<a id="what-a-rehearsal-could-help-you-investigate"></a>

## What a rehearsal could help you investigate

Consider three decisions and the questions they leave open.

<a id="understanding-an-audience"></a>

### Understanding an audience

A creator rehearsal could explore *what quiet subscribers might stop finding useful*, then identify questions for real audience research.

<a id="workplace-decisions"></a>

### Workplace decisions

A rollout rehearsal could expose *which explanation creates an objection*, with its assumptions and simulated evidence available to inspect.

<a id="access-and-public-services"></a>

### Access and public services

A policy rehearsal could reveal *whose access might become harder* and which field evidence would test that concern.

## Questions about RawFish

**Which decisions can I rehearse?**

Creator scenarios explore how posts and announcements might reach loyal followers, new viewers, critics and quiet audiences. Internal policy looks at how managers communicate workplace changes and where a rollout could stall. Public policy looks at capacity, unequal effects and incentives. RawFish uses representative group types and excludes simulations of identifiable people and sensitive workplace sources. The [rehearsal guide](https://rawthoughts.in/rawfish/rehearse-a-decision/) explains which scenarios and sources fit.

**What do I receive from a rehearsal?**

The scenario, settings, risks and questions for field investigation. Read, Ask and Analysis help you tell what came from source material, what agents did during the run and what the report inferred. A generated persona cannot speak for a real person or population. The [findings guide](https://rawthoughts.in/rawfish/read-the-findings/) explains how to distinguish a report’s claims from their supporting evidence.

**Can I use a local model?**

Choose a local model or explicitly select an external provider. Before running, read Preflight’s model-readiness result, scale, latency and available cost estimate. Choose graph memory separately; a local report model alone does not tell you where memory sends data.

**How can I compare runs?**

Keep the conditions aligned, change the proposal or random seed, and compare the separate runs. A saved run retains its own settings and report. The [findings guide](https://rawthoughts.in/rawfish/read-the-findings/) explains how to compare the evidence behind a difference.

**What could follow if a decision were taken?**

A rehearsal tries a direction before it is taken, so that you can see what could follow if the decision were taken: a reaction you did not expect, a constraint the plan neglected, a question for the people affected. These are questions to investigate, not predictions, and a generated persona cannot speak for a real person. Comparing a direction taken with one not taken is what RawFish is designed around: it varies one thing you declare in the same simulated world and compares what follows. Today a second version of a proposal gets its own scenario and report beside the first, and the comparison is the next piece to build. The findings go back to the people deciding. [Weave can link a rehearsal to the proposal it tested](https://rawthoughts.in/weave/review-an-observation/) as evidence while the run and its report stay with RawFish, and the reviewer still decides.

**Where can I explore the underlying questions?**

[Rehearsal and evidence](https://rawthoughts.in/collections/systems/rehearsal-and-evidence/) develops the interaction mechanisms, quiet audiences and kinds of variation a simulation needs to make understandable.

**Where should I start?**

Start with the [rehearsal guide](https://rawthoughts.in/rawfish/rehearse-a-decision/). It follows a workplace decision from proposal to findings and needs nothing installed, because the rehearsal it describes is the rebuild's design and the retained prototype covers only part of it. The prototype guides also read without its code. Only running the reference prototype needs a source checkout, and its developer walkthrough covers that.

**What does the reference prototype do?**

Its six-step cockpit takes a scenario through saved settings to a generated report. [Understand the reference prototype](https://rawthoughts.in/rawfish/use-the-local-prototype/) follows a creator announcement and explains what its results can support. Developers can use the [local walkthrough](https://docs.rawthoughts.in/rawfish/using) for startup and configuration.
