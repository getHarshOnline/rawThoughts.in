# Gazette

Gazette carries the work outward as publications. Write a body of thought from the whole to its parts: a collection develops one question, a subcollection takes up a branch of it, a journey sets the order an argument needs, and a crossing lets a reader follow the thought into another inquiry. Gazette declares and checks that structure, so readers, search and machines find the work the way you wrote it. A tool such as Astro or Hugo groups pages; Gazette gives each grouping a meaning it carries through the whole publication.

The writing, research and documentation stay with the projects that own them. Gazette compiles the sources you choose into one publication or several connected ones, each with its own address and design. [Create your first publication](https://rawthoughts.in/gazette/first-publication/) to start writing locally, edit an article and preview the result.

<a id="one-structure-followed-by-readers-and-machines"></a>

## One structure, followed by readers and machines

Give a reader a way into the work. Group articles around a question, put a series in reading order, or link from one publication to another. The theme shapes each page. Search, feeds and machine-readable files use the same titles, descriptions, addresses and declared relationships.

Among [the products](https://rawthoughts.in/products/), Gazette publishes a body of thought: it is the act that goes out once its publisher has approved the work. It publishes for a person directly today and takes its sources from their owners; [Pale](https://rawthoughts.in/pale/) calling it and [Weave](https://rawthoughts.in/weave/) supplying an organisation's facts are the two connections in front.

## From a question to a connected publication

Take a question a reader brings, such as how a product's design led to its guide. The pieces that develop it, the order that carries it and the crossing into another inquiry are the reader's way through. Gazette gives the editor, command line and connected agents the same account of that structure; [write in VS Code](https://rawthoughts.in/gazette/write-with-feedback/) or [work with an agent](https://rawthoughts.in/gazette/work-with-an-agent/) to see it from the author's side.

Each publication's theme gives it a world, a visual language: this site uses Carrier, for applied systems and consulting, and [MONAD's research](https://causality.in/monad/) uses Lattice, for research and exact notation. Follow the link to see how one thought can cross between distinct publications. [Choose a reading form](https://rawthoughts.in/gazette/change-presentation/) explains the shared foundation and the worlds built on it.

1. 
   
   <a id="start-from-the-question"></a>
   
   ### Start from the question
   
   Name what the writing is trying to answer. A collection holds the pieces that develop it, and a subcollection takes up a branch. An article that stands alone can stay on its own, with links from the discussions it helps.

2. 
   
   <a id="develop-it-in-pieces"></a>
   
   ### Develop it in pieces
   
   Keep each argument with its notes, mathematics and supporting resources, written once. A piece has one home, and other discussions can lead readers into it without copying it. Then choose which sources to publish, where the pages live and which theme gives them their look.

3. 
   
   <a id="set-the-order-that-carries-it"></a>
   
   ### Set the order that carries it
   
   When a later piece builds on an earlier one, make them a journey so the reader always has the next step and the way back. Shared keywords alone do not make an order.

4. 
   
   <a id="cross-into-another-inquiry"></a>
   
   ### Cross into another inquiry
   
   A link can leave for another publication. Gazette knows it is a crossing, so the reader stays on the essay while the theme borrows the destination's look. When you are ready to see the result, preview it as you save with [your first publication](https://rawthoughts.in/gazette/first-publication/), follow your links in the preview to check that the article and its search entry agree, and [export a copy of the build](https://rawthoughts.in/gazette/review-and-deliver/) from the command line or through your agent for review.

## What a publication needs to preserve

<a id="arguments-and-supporting-detail"></a>

### Arguments and supporting detail

A derivation can stay beside the article *without interrupting everyone* who only needs the main argument.

<a id="branches-order-and-crossings"></a>

### Branches, order and crossings

A reader should be able to tell a branch of the question from a step in the argument and a link into another inquiry, and correcting an article's title should update *the page, search results and related reading together*.

<a id="guides-and-technical-reference"></a>

### Guides and technical reference

A guide and API reference need to agree about the system, even when they need *different reading forms*.

## Questions about Gazette

**Who is Gazette for?**

Authors, researchers, engineers and publishing teams working with connected material: a single site, several publications, or a publication alongside product documentation. Agents can help author and operate it without becoming the source of its meaning or visual style.

**What can a publication contain?**

Articles, collections, ordered journeys, product help and landing pages. Add equations, diagrams, saved notebook results and notes where they help explain the work. A theme can supply interactive figures. Developer guides and generated API or library reference can be [built for Mintlify](https://rawthoughts.in/gazette/publish-developer-documentation/).

**What does the theme decide?**

The theme keeps meaning and appearance separate: it controls layout, type, colour and interactions. Gazette tells it what the page contains, where it belongs and where its links go. A theme can preview a linked publication's look or make a figure interactive while the article still reads without JavaScript or motion.

**How can one article serve several reading paths?**

Readers arrive with different questions, so Gazette lets several discussions lead into one article while it keeps a single home. Navigation, related reading and search use those declared connections. A collection develops a shared question; a journey adds an order within one. Linking to an independent article gives readers another way into the work without copying it.

**How is Gazette different from Astro or Hugo?**

Gazette carries the structure of a body of thought, which a static-site generator leaves to the author. [Astro's content collections](https://docs.astro.build/en/guides/content-collections/) supply loaders, schemas and queries for structured content. [Hugo](https://gohugo.io/content-management/organization/) organizes pages through sections and page bundles. Gazette makes the relationships between publications part of its publishing model: an article's home, an ordered journey and a link into another site have declared meanings that it checks and carries into navigation and machine-readable outputs. The workspace chooses the sources and publications; themes give them their appearance. [From one site to many](https://rawthoughts.in/gazette/from-one-site-to-many/) follows one small writing project as it grows into connected publications.

**Where should I start?**

Start on your own machine. Gazette builds a publication from the sources you choose and rebuilds its local preview as you save. [Create your first publication](https://rawthoughts.in/gazette/first-publication/) takes you from an article to a browser preview. When it is ready, [export the build](https://rawthoughts.in/gazette/review-and-deliver/) for your publishing destination.
