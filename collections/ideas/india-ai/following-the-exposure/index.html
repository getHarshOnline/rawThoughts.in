Own the bridge, not the trophy

India's AI crossing

AI is the spark; India is the circuit. The wager is that India's long arcs of consumption and infrastructure will be tested by an unusually demanding new customer. If electricity, permits, networks and skills keep up, the investment cycle can pay for useful capacity. If they do not, the same observations should force the thesis to change.

This is the explanatory edition of an argument begun on 17 September 2025. It follows the economic exposure and the rules for testing it, without prescribing a list of securities or a personal allocation. Bubble or bridge? supplied the provocation. The task here is to connect the thesis to what a financial claim actually owns.

The fund's name fits the story. Its holdings need not. A convincing account of AI's infrastructure needs can lead to a product that owns businesses with only a small claim on that activity, alongside businesses with no established connection at all. The brochure carries the whole transformation; the money buys particular securities.

Begin with buyers, then follow the wires

Households pay rent, buy insurance, service loans and decide whether another application is useful enough to purchase. Their income is part of the infrastructure thesis because somebody eventually has to buy what the infrastructure makes possible. Savings inflows and formal payroll can help investigate that capacity, provided their definitions survive the journey into the argument.

My proposed buyer test joined a SIP stoppage ratio of at least 75 percent for three months with weakening EPFO additions in Maharashtra, Karnataka and Tamil Nadu. This was a research trigger, not an observed recession or an instruction that a particular investment must fall. A same-month ratio of completed or terminated SIP registrations to new registrations is not a cohort failure rate; EPFO net additions include exits and returning subscribers. A reconciliation can move the first series without a change in household distress. The household argument keeps the historical numbers and their denominators together.

The physical test is different. Watch tenders become awards and awards become commissioned supply. CEA's projected 82.37 GWh of system storage in 2026–27 and 411.4 GWh in 2031–32 describe a requirement, not a completed conveyor. The June 2025 announcement of support for another 30 GWh of batteries mattered because it could help finance that conversion. By July 2026 that tranche had letters of award for 22.43 GWh and financial closure for 5 GWh.1 The applicable transmission-waiver category, connection and operating arrangement still decide whether an individual project benefits. The operating requirements follow those distinctions.

The third test is inside the data centre. My proposed floor was 1.5 GW of operational IT load in India by 2027. It was a way to challenge an assumed pace of adoption, not an official target. Campus power, IT load, announced capacity and an operator's development pipeline must not be pooled into one progress line. An operator's projected capacity is also not the national total. A revised forecast for another fiscal year or market segment cannot silently replace the denominator. The government reported 1.57 GW of installed data-centre power capacity for August 2026.2 The 1.5 GW floor counts operational IT load in 2027.

Scarcity elsewhere creates an opportunity, not a destination

The opportunity is easier to see where other grids are saying no, or at least not yet. PJM's 2025 planning forecast incorporated substantial new data-centre demand.3 PJM's January 2026 forecast counted large loads only once they had firm commitments, which lowered the near-term peak. It projected 3.6 percent annual growth over ten years.4 The practical consequence is a need for generation, transmission and credible information about which proposed loads will materialise. It does not mean every inquiry will become a connected campus.

Ireland's 2025 consultation tied new large-user connections to security of supply and network constraints.5 Its December decision added specific requirements including additional renewable supply and assessment of the application's location on the network.6 Singapore's Green Data Centre Roadmap aimed to unlock at least 300 MW of additional capacity, with more through green energy.7 Scarcity did not vanish; it became a condition attached to expansion.

India gains an option if it can deliver power, routes, approvals and a useful service on a schedule. It does not inherit the demand merely because another country has a queue. The customer may accept the delay, change its workload or choose another location.

Training and inference also pull on different parts of the map. Large training runs may favour firm power and connected blocks. Interactive inference may value proximity to users and data. GCC tenancy can support offices and local services without making the landlord an owner of the tenant's computing revenue. Geography suggests a relationship to investigate; the contract tells us what the relationship pays.

The shelf does not match the blueprint

The original investment question was whether capital could reach the enabling businesses before a successful buildout became obvious. That requires a more exact account than calling every nearby business an AI beneficiary.

Intended exposure How a broad product can drift What to inspect
Transmission, substations and equipment An infrastructure basket can include telecom and integrated energy alongside the desired suppliers Holdings, revenue mix and the contracts actually tied to the buildout
Electrical equipment and automation A manufacturing basket also includes autos and basic materials The share attributable to the relevant equipment, with unknowns left visible
Freight, ports and warehousing A transport product can be dominated by vehicle manufacturers Whether the claim follows freight throughput or vehicle sales
Copper and aluminium for electrification A metals basket can be dominated by steel The actual commodities, customers and pricing exposure
Offices used by GCCs and related businesses A property label can be mistaken for direct data-centre ownership Tenant mix, leases, operating costs and whether any computing claim exists
Household credit and insurance A financial-services label can bundle very different underwriting and funding risks Which household activity supplies the income and how stress reaches it

Defence demand, for example, can have a policy-driven logic without being a pure expression of the AI infrastructure thesis. A diversifier should remain a diversifier rather than acquire a convenient story after purchase. Similarly, an upstream supplier and a downstream assembler can face very different customer concentration and margin pressure even when both appear in a technology basket.

A better product could narrow the mismatch. Until then, measuring the mismatch is more useful than pretending the shelf contains the ideal instrument. The method should remain able to replace a weak proxy when a clearer one appears, rather than become loyal to the first wrapper that carried the label.

A holdings percentage is not a revenue percentage

Consider a hypothetical, fully invested fund with four holdings. These are invented businesses and weights, not recommendations or observations of an actual product. The revenue-share column gives the stipulated share of each business’s revenue from the stated relationship.

Holding Weight in fund Relevant operating relationship Relevant revenue share
Power-equipment supplier 30% Supplies equipment used by computing campuses and other customers 20%
Construction business 25% Builds suitable facilities alongside other buildings 10%
Network provider 20% Sells a declared class of computing-site connections 40%
Office-property owner 25% Owns nearby offices, with no established campus-computing claim No exposure assigned

The first three holdings constitute 75 percent of the fund. That is a broad holdings classification, not 75 percent exposure to AI revenue. If wiw_i is a holding's fund weight and rir_i its stipulated relevant revenue fraction, the descriptive look-through proxy is:

L=∑iwiri=0.30×0.20+0.25×0.10+0.20×0.40=0.165. L=\sum_i w_i r_i =0.30\times0.20+0.25\times0.10+0.20\times0.40 =0.165.

The result is 16.5 percent under the chosen classifications. It is not an estimate of the fund's return sensitivity: revenue mix, margins, debt, valuation and the timing of each contract can produce different sensitivities. The office holding contributes no assigned exposure to this calculation; its absence is a classification boundary, not proof that its business can never change.

Nor does the office holding become an AI asset because it is geographically close to one. Missing exposure evidence can remain unassigned. If the businesses do not disclose a useful revenue breakdown, the honest map may have ranges or unknowns rather than an invented precise percentage. The infrastructure thesis can survive while its proposed investment proxy becomes much less convincing.

Two wrappers can hold one business

Now suppose two illustrative funds each assign 30 percent to the same equipment supplier. Splitting a hypothetical allocation equally between them still leaves 30 percent of the combined position in that supplier: half of 30 plus half of 30. The two fund names have not created two independent business exposures. The SEC's investor-education account of diversification makes this practical distinction: several funds can share holdings, and a narrowly focused fund need not supply the diversification its wrapper suggests.

Different securities can also depend on the same underlying event. Two suppliers may share a customer's construction schedule, the same imported component or the same financing cycle. A holdings-overlap calculation can detect repeated names; the operating map is needed to detect those repeated dependencies. Neither alone is a complete risk model. A disruption can reach several different claims through one shared path.

Three gates, three different revisions

A portfolio is a hypothesis with a stopwatch. The useful part of the original rules is that different failures change different links; they do not all justify the same reflex.

Proposed gate Observation to investigate Revision to the argument
A: the wires Tender-to-award-to-commissioning progress slips by two quarters against declared milestones Lower confidence in near-term delivery; separate enabling equipment demand from a campus expected to operate soon
B: operating capacity Comparable operational IT load remains below the proposed 1.5 GW floor in 2027 Revisit the assumed pace of local demand and the link from computing to a particular landlord or supplier
C: the buyer The proposed three-month SIP threshold coincides with weakening state payroll in Maharashtra, Karnataka and Tamil Nadu Investigate household income and demand; reduce confidence in growth assumptions that require those buyers to remain unaffected

Note

These thresholds date from September 2025. They are neither back-tested trading rules nor proof of causality. The observation window, comparable series and explanation for a revision should be recorded before a favourable later event tempts us to rewrite the test.

The sequencing matters. Equipment suppliers can receive orders before a campus starts producing; commissioning can arrive after a security's price has already anticipated it. Being early to an actual dependency and being early to a fashionable label are different things. Conversely, a bad quarter may be temporary without making every delay harmless. The gate asks which part of the expected sequence moved and whether that changes the claim being held.

The favourable case needs discipline too. Suppose inference expands faster than expected. That can strengthen demand for regional capacity while leaving a particular fund poorly exposed to it. A good story does not repair a weak proxy, and a higher price does not establish a better prospective return.

The instrument can move while the thesis stands

A fund can rebalance, a company can change its business mix, and a relevant contract can end. The thesis may be unchanged while the selected instrument drifts away from it. Conversely, a stable holding can acquire a more direct exposure as a new activity grows. The snapshot therefore has a date. Comparing like-for-like snapshots reveals that movement without assuming that every change in the fund's price came from the AI story.

The original method called for a scheduled review each September and after material methodology or holdings changes. Pull the current factsheets, classify the holdings against the intended economic activity, preserve the classification reasons and recompute the look-through. Review overlap at the same time. A range or an unassigned share is preferable to invented certainty when disclosures do not support a narrower estimate.

There are two different calculations to keep apart. The share of fund holdings classified into target industries measures broad thesis capture. Weighting those holdings again by their relevant revenue fractions, as in the 16.5 percent example, gives a narrower descriptive proxy. Neither is a model of return sensitivity, and the second is only as good as the revenue classifications.

Cash-flow planning sits beside this method. A long investment horizon does not prevent a household from needing money before a project matures. A separate liquidity reserve can prevent a temporary funding need from forcing a sale, but its size and instrument belong to that household's circumstances. A projection with regular contributions and occasional withdrawals is a scenario, not a promised compounding path. Calendar discipline cannot manufacture income when the payslip stops.

Tax and payout labels need the same care. A distribution can contain different components; a historical exemption can have conditions or later amendments. Calling an instrument ballast describes a proposed role, not guaranteed liquidity, price stability or tax treatment. Those details must be checked for the actual claim and date rather than inherited from an old portfolio table.

What survives the test?

If the grid sprints and storage lags, the campus's round-the-clock proposition weakens even while some equipment demand remains. If buildings outrun substations, construction is not operational capacity. If household income cracks, lower production costs do not by themselves restore the customer. If the services arrive earlier than expected, the question is still who owns the resulting claim and what price already assumes that success.

The operating and payment maps separate necessity from retained value. The stress test then asks whether those links survive changes in access, currency and physical supply. One Yardstick, One Clock and The Red Pill supply the wider question about capability, reusable intelligence and who can buy the resulting output.

My aim is to move a rupee closer to the thesis it is supposed to express. That starts with a real dependency, follows the business and contract, checks the instrument and then asks what is already priced in. Necessary infrastructure, genuine business benefit, suitable exposure and attractive price are four separate propositions.

Don't chase the trophy because it has AI engraved on it. Understand the bridge it must cross. Bring a ruler.
