Title: Testing India's AI crossing

[Read HTML](https://rawthoughts.in/collections/ideas/india-ai/testing-indias-ai-crossing/)

Author: Harsh Joshi

India can build something substantial during a boom and still distribute its benefits badly. A campus can create useful capacity while a worker loses an opening, a supplier waits for payment or a household gains little room to spend. “Bubble or bridge?” becomes a useful question only when those outcomes can coexist inside the same account.

The argument starts with that tension and follows the physical and commercial chain that might resolve it. Electricity must reach equipment, heat must leave, a customer must obtain a usable allocation, and contracts must carry a claim on the completed work. Each step adds a possible constraint. Each also changes who bears the risk when access, prices or payment conditions change.

The national story becomes more demanding at this scale. A necessary supplier is not automatically the greatest beneficiary; a domestic campus can remain exposed to imported equipment or an external allocation rule. The point of testing the chain is to discover which promises survive those dependencies, not to turn every dependency into a reason to abandon the opportunity.

For an individual buyer, the same chain becomes a question about [useful compute](https://rawthoughts.in/collections/useful-compute/). Beyond the campus, [production, ownership and income](https://post-agi-economics.org/collections/thinking-gets-cheap/who-gets-the-gain/from-capability-to-economic-consequence/) determine how a productive gain reaches the people who need it.
