# Bubble or bridge?

Oracle's September 2025 surge made the AI boom feel almost absurdly large. Hundreds of billions of dollars could appear in a company's market value while the machinery behind the promise would take years to build. I reached for Finland's annual GDP as a comparison of scale. A market valuation is a stock and GDP is a year's flow, so they are not interchangeable quantities. That was precisely the vertigo: the market was pricing a future before the future had acquired its substations.

The closing-day increase was roughly $240 billion, rather than a settled $300 billion addition.<sup><a id="fnref-note-6f7261636c652d636c6f7365"></a>[1](#fn-note-6f7261636c652d636c6f7365)</sup> The separate computing agreement was another number: OpenAI's subsequent September announcement described its Oracle partnership as exceeding $300 billion over five years, following July's agreement to develop 4.5 GW of additional capacity in the United States.<sup><a id="fnref-note-6f70656e61692d7374617267617465"></a>[2](#fn-note-6f70656e61692d7374617267617465)</sup> In September 2026 Oracle reported $664 billion of remaining performance obligations, a measure of contracted work still to deliver.<sup><a id="fnref-note-6f7261636c652d32303236"></a>[3](#fn-note-6f7261636c652d32303236)</sup> Neither a contract value nor a planned gigawatt is a completed job, a paid bill or an operating Indian data centre.

Who pays for a commitment on that scale, and when? Construction needs financing before the capacity earns revenue. A large contract can help secure that financing while leaving the builder dependent on the buyer's ability to keep paying. That was the question behind the September 2025 excitement: can the froth pay for a bridge that remains useful after it subsides?

For India, that bridge has two ends. One is productive capacity: electricity, storage, data centres, applications and people able to use them. The other is purchasing power. A country can acquire more capacity while the households expected to buy its output become less secure. The interesting question is what connects the two.

<a id="the-employment-headline-contains-several-stories"></a>

## The employment headline contains several stories

The Employees’ Provident Fund Organisation (EPFO) records participation in its provident-fund system through administrative subscriber and payroll records. Its June 2025 release reported 21.89 lakh net member additions, including 10.62 lakh first-time subscribers. It separately recorded 16.93 lakh returning members. Exits enter the net calculation too. These are provisional administrative counts, revised as records arrive; 21.89 lakh cannot simply be relabelled newly created jobs.

Among first-time subscribers, 60.22 percent were aged 18–25. That denominator matters: the figure does not describe 60 percent of all net additions. Expert services contributed 42.14 percent of net membership, with manpower suppliers accounting for about half of that category. Security services and contractors also appeared in it.<sup><a id="fnref-note-6570666f"></a>[4](#fn-note-6570666f)</sup> The composition makes the labour question more specific, but an administrative category is not a measure of how many tasks AI can replace.

My concern is about the kinds of work through which a young person first enters formal employment. If routine processing, support and coordination become cheaper to automate, where does the next rung of the ladder appear? A rise in formal participation can coexist with vulnerability in the work being formalised. Equally, a vulnerable task does not establish that a whole job will disappear. Employers can change the bundle, expand demand or use the same people differently.

Women's participation sharpens this question. The Periodic Labour Force Survey (PLFS), a household survey of employment and unemployment, shows all-India female labour-force participation, for people aged 15 and above under usual status, rising from 23.3 percent in 2017–18 to 37.0 percent in 2022–23 and 41.7 percent in 2023–24. The rural series is different: 24.6, 41.5 and 47.6 percent respectively.<sup><a id="fnref-note-706c6673"></a>[5](#fn-note-706c6673)</sup> The survey was redesigned in January 2025, and its first calendar-year report put 2025 at 40.0 percent all-India and 45.9 percent rural. MoSPI asks users to account for that redesign when comparing with those July–June years.<sup><a id="fnref-note-706c66732d32303235"></a>[6](#fn-note-706c66732d32303235)</sup> Participation also does not tell us, by itself, whether the additional work is secure, well paid or salaried.

There is a plausible shock absorber. Global Capability Centres had more than 1.9 million employees and over 1,700 centres in FY24; their reported revenue was $64.6 billion. The Nasscom–Zinnov estimate for FY26 is 2,117 centres, 2.36 million people and $98.4 billion of revenue.<sup><a id="fnref-note-6763632d32303236"></a>[7](#fn-note-6763632d32303236)</sup> Their work can extend beyond back-office processing into engineering, products, analytics and research. The projection towards roughly 2.8 million professionals by 2030 describes a possible expansion, not an automatic placement service for everyone displaced elsewhere.<sup><a id="fnref-note-676363"></a>[8](#fn-note-676363)</sup> Skills, wages, cities and timing still have to meet.

<a id="the-woman-behind-the-sip-chart"></a>

## The woman behind the SIP chart

Imagine a woman working in business-process management in Mumbai Suburban, earning ₹42,000 a month. Her salary supports rent, household expenses, perhaps an EMI and a monthly systematic investment plan. The SIP is a claim on a future she expects her present income to keep funding.

Now suppose her contract is not renewed. She pauses the SIP. A market dashboard records an interruption; her household records a missing payslip. The decision need not express panic about equity prices. It may be the sensible consequence of needing the money this month.

That is the connection I want to investigate, including among households earning ₹30,000–₹60,000 a month in places such as Uttar Pradesh. It is a proposed line of inquiry, not a claim that those households share one budget or that a national financial series identifies their motives. A district-level comparison would need appropriate survey weights, sample sizes, occupation definitions and a period matched to the employment change. A colourful city chart cannot supply those missing joins.

The national savings figures were strong. AMFI's series records SIP contributions of ₹27,269 crore in June 2025 and ₹28,464 crore in July. June had 8.64 crore contributing accounts and SIP assets of ₹15.31 lakh crore; July had 9.11 crore accounts and ₹15.19 lakh crore.<sup><a id="fnref-note-616d6669"></a>[9](#fn-note-616d6669)</sup> Accounts are also not unique people or households, and assets change with markets as well as contributions.

The much-discussed stoppage ratio has another denominator problem. Terminated or completed SIP registrations divided by new registrations in the same month is a flow ratio. It is not the fraction of a tracked cohort that failed, and it is not a household unemployment rate. Scheduled completions, switching and reconciliations can affect it. A ratio around three-quarters may deserve investigation without meaning that three-quarters of investors abandoned investing.

The useful hypothesis is joint: sustained interruption in savings alongside deterioration in the relevant employment series could reveal pressure on the buyer. The inference needs corroboration from household income and expenditure, not a confident story attached to two national lines.

<a id="buildings-need-a-working-system-around-them"></a>

## Buildings need a working system around them

AI's investment cycle could finance useful infrastructure even if some investors overpay. But a campus announcement can get ahead of its electrical connection, storage, cooling, fibre and customer. The Indian opportunity is the conversion of all those promises into an operating system of production.

The scale is easier to grasp with units. A hypothetical 100 MW IT load operating at a power usage effectiveness of 1.3 requires 130 MW at the facility boundary. At constant load that is 3.12 GWh per day. An eight-hour interval requires 1.04 GWh delivered to that load, before allowing for storage losses, reserve or degradation. The corresponding example for 1 MW of IT load is 10.4 MWh over eight hours. Watts and watt-hours cannot trade places because a policy document needs a compact slogan.

India reached 50 percent non-fossil installed electricity capacity in June 2025.<sup><a id="fnref-note-6d6e7265"></a>[10](#fn-note-6d6e7265)</sup> That is an important capacity milestone, not a statement that half of every hour's electricity was supplied by those sources. By July 2026 the capacity share had passed 54 percent, and in 2025–26 non-fossil sources generated 29.2 percent of India's electricity.<sup><a id="fnref-note-6e6f6e666f7373696c2d32303236"></a>[11](#fn-note-6e6f6e666f7373696c2d32303236)</sup> The 500 GW non-fossil ambition similarly needs transmission, dispatch and firm supply behind it. A training run cannot negotiate with sunset.

CEA's National Electricity Plan projected a system-wide storage requirement of 82.37 GWh in 2026–27 and 411.4 GWh in 2031–32. These totals combine pumped storage and batteries. The battery portions were 34.72 and 236.22 GWh respectively.<sup><a id="fnref-note-6e65702d73746f72616765"></a>[12](#fn-note-6e65702d73746f72616765)</sup> They are planning requirements for the electricity system, not commissioned capacity or a dedicated allowance for AI.

The June 2025 announcement of additional support for 30 GWh of batteries and transmission-charge relief made the direction more credible.<sup><a id="fnref-note-73746f726167652d737570706f7274"></a>[13](#fn-note-73746f726167652d737570706f7274)</sup> By July 2026 that tranche had letters of award for 22.43 GWh and financial closure for 5 GWh. On 4 November 2026 the Cabinet approved support for a further 92 GWh of storage, 30 GWh of it batteries.<sup><a id="fnref-note-7667662d32303236"></a>[14](#fn-note-7667662d32303236)</sup> The legal category still matters. Later official accounts distinguish co-located batteries eligible for the June 2028 window from other battery configurations whose waivers taper, while pumped-storage eligibility turns on the construction award date.<sup><a id="fnref-note-7761697665722d63617465676f72696573"></a>[15](#fn-note-7761697665722d63617465676f72696573)</sup> The general phrase “storage waiver to 2028” is too broad for a project's economics.

The sequence is tender, award, financing, construction, commissioning and sustained service. Each transition can fail. Cheap solar helps; it does not make the rest of the sequence free.

<a id="applications-first-can-be-a-serious-strategy"></a>

## Applications first can be a serious strategy

Training and inference need not settle in the same place. A large training job may favour a contiguous cluster with firm power, data access and a predictable operating window. An interactive service may place more value on proximity to users, the location of its data and tail latency. These are workload-dependent trade-offs, not a rule that all training must leave India or every inference request must stay nearby.

The July Oracle agreement concerned US capacity. Nebius's September 2025 Microsoft agreement concerned dedicated capacity at Vineland, New Jersey. Those announcements showed large buyers contracting for future supply; they did not establish an eastward migration into India. Nebius's own disclosure made delivery and availability consequential to the agreement.<sup><a id="fnref-note-6e6562697573"></a>[16](#fn-note-6e6562697573)</sup> In November 2026 Nebius expected to have delivered more than half of that New Jersey capacity by the end of the year and scheduled the rest for the second quarter of 2027, after a delay at a third-party site.<sup><a id="fnref-note-6e65626975732d32303236"></a>[17](#fn-note-6e65626975732d32303236)</sup> The same letter expected Nebius's first sites in the Asia-Pacific region, two colocations in India, to come online in 2027.

India can build capabilities through applications while the harder infrastructure catches up. That means useful agricultural advice, lower input waste, logistics, credit decisions people can contest, better tools for small businesses and training tied to actual work. It is not a consolation prize for failing to announce the largest model.

I also see a role for governance in making the transition manageable: clarity about accountability, safety, procurement and recourse can let adoption proceed without pretending that every institution is ready. Treating that as a buffer is my strategic interpretation. It is not evidence that the government designed regulation to delay automation or that compliance automatically protects a job. A buffer earns its keep only if the time is used to build capability.

There is now a concrete later example. In March 2026, Sarvam reported training its 30B and 105B models in India on IndiaAI compute and releasing the weights alongside production services.<sup><a id="fnref-note-73617276616d"></a>[18](#fn-note-73617276616d)</sup> That is identifiable work beyond an announced GPU count. It does not establish the conversion rate for the entire programme. [The compute-number investigation](https://rawthoughts.in/collections/ideas/india-ai/what-the-compute-numbers-count/) follows that distinction in detail.

<a id="concentration-at-the-top-a-thin-buyer-at-the-bottom"></a>

## Concentration at the top, a thin buyer at the bottom

The boom has a two-sided demand problem. At one end, a small number of very large buyers can support enormous equipment and capacity orders. At the other, a broad population must have enough income to buy the applications and services eventually produced. A strong contract at the top does not settle demand at the bottom.

Nor is a GPU an immortal bridge pier. Equipment ages, workloads change and new machines alter the economics. A five-year depreciation schedule is an accounting assumption where a company uses it, not a universal physical expiry date. It nevertheless reminds us that financing long-lived buildings and financing rapidly changing compute are different bets. A building may remain after its first equipment cycle; whether it remains useful depends on its power, cooling and ability to accommodate the next one.

There is an older Indian investment question inside this new one. The Economic Survey 2023–24 noted that private non-financial gross fixed capital formation in machinery, equipment and intellectual-property products grew cumulatively by 35 percent in current prices between FY19 and FY23, against 105 percent for dwellings, other buildings and structures.<sup><a id="fnref-note-65636f6e6f6d69632d737572766579"></a>[19](#fn-note-65636f6e6f6d69632d737572766579)</sup> That comparison concerns a particular period and price basis. It asks whether capital is building productive capability as well as physical property.

Progress in poverty reduction matters too, but its evidence has to retain its form. NITI Aayog's multidimensional-poverty series puts 2015–16 at 24.85 percent and projects 2022–23 at 11.28 percent; the latter is extrapolated rather than a direct survey observation for that year.<sup><a id="fnref-note-6e6974692d6d7069"></a>[20](#fn-note-6e6974692d6d7069)</sup> Neither figure is disposable monthly income. Leaving multidimensional poverty is a major change without necessarily producing the spare cash to absorb a technology-led employment interruption.

This is why productive investment and the buyer's income belong in the same argument. Raising agricultural productivity, reducing logistics waste and helping workers acquire useful capabilities can broaden the gain. Replacing labour may raise output per worker while narrowing the income channel through which some households buy that output. The distribution is part of the mechanism, not an afterthought to the productivity chart.

<a id="what-would-change-my-mind"></a>

## What would change my mind?

A thesis needs observations that can push back. In September 2025 I proposed three kinds of tests. They are research triggers, not observed results or automatic causal diagnoses.

| Proposed test | What it would challenge | What would still need checking |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A stoppage ratio around 70–75 percent or higher for three months alongside weakening EPFO additions in Maharashtra, Karnataka and Tamil Nadu | The resilience of the urban buyer supporting the story | Reconciliations, subscriber composition, household income and whether the same people are affected |
| Repeated double-digit declines in comparable property registrations in Bengaluru or Pune over consecutive quarters | The assumption that employment and property demand are absorbing the transition smoothly | Base effects, local rules, prices, supply and the relevant buyer cohort |
| India below a proposed 1.5 GW operational IT-load floor in 2027 while more than 80 percent of a consistently defined new AI-capacity pipeline goes to the US, Europe and Middle East | The expectation that global scarcity quickly redirects substantial capacity into India | A common capacity boundary, geography, announcement versus operation and the observation window |

By October 2026 the savings half of the first test had its reading. AMFI's stoppage ratio was at or above 75 percent in every month from February to October 2026 and reached 116.7 percent in October, when 59.12 lakh registrations were discontinued or completed against 50.68 lakh new ones; contributions stayed above ₹32,000 crore a month from August to October.<sup><a id="fnref-note-616d66692d32303236"></a>[21](#fn-note-616d66692d32303236)</sup> The ratio counts registrations that completed their tenure among those discontinued. The test also asks for weakening EPFO additions in Maharashtra, Karnataka and Tamil Nadu. The government reported 1.57 GW of installed data-centre power capacity for August 2026.<sup><a id="fnref-note-64632d32303236"></a>[22](#fn-note-64632d32303236)</sup> The 1.5 GW floor counts operational IT load in 2027.

A later forecast with a different market boundary or fiscal year cannot silently score an earlier test. The next essays follow [the operating requirements](https://rawthoughts.in/collections/ideas/india-ai/testing-indias-ai-crossing/from-posters-to-throughput/) and [the claims on the resulting value](https://rawthoughts.in/collections/ideas/india-ai/testing-indias-ai-crossing/tracing-the-infrastructure-story/).

[One Yardstick, One Clock](https://post-agi-economics.org/blog/one-yardstick-one-clock/) (on Post-AGI Economics) supplies the wider lens: value, evidence and actuators must meet before a capability becomes an economic substitute. Institutions and integration create a window in which to prepare. Who retains the surplus then determines who can keep buying. The destination of that project is [Post-AGI Economics](https://post-agi-economics.org/) (on Post-AGI Economics), where the capacity to produce and the capacity to purchase are studied together.

The bridge is real only if it reaches both banks. I want to see payslips in Pune, a sustainable SIP in Mumbai, a young worker able to afford a room in Bengaluru and firm power in Tamil Nadu, as well as chips inside a campus. Froth may help finance the crossing. The foundation is what lets people keep using it.
