Title: Practice

[Read HTML](https://rawthoughts.in/collections/practice/)

Author: Harsh Joshi

Where have we actually got to? One person is waiting for a decision, another believes their work is finished, and the customer still needs a usable result. They can all be describing the same piece of work accurately. Before choosing the next step, we need to understand whose part is complete and what the others still need.

Knowing who is responsible matters as much as knowing what happened. An account relationship can continue through a failed opportunity; the person maintaining it may have no authority to promise the next delivery. A handover can change who takes the work forward without changing who did it. Those connections help explain why a single owner or completion flag can leave people with quite different expectations.

Applications introduce another kind of uncertainty. A missing reply may leave an action's outcome unknown; a successful delivery may leave its usefulness disputed. Recovery begins by finding out what happened and which responsibility remains. The cases here explore those moments through their particular circumstances, so a practical arrangement can be judged by the work it actually carries.
