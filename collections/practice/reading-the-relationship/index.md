# The relationship inside the account

A customer has two opportunities in progress: one team is exploring a new service while another renews an existing engagement. Someone maintains continuity across the account, someone handles each opportunity, and someone else can promise delivery. Which of them is "the owner"?

An organisation chart cannot answer that question alone. The useful unit is the particular relationship: what somebody may promise, why the customer is interested, who carries the next responsibility and who can accept its result. A commercial system becomes more intelligible when those relationships have room to be recorded in their own right, instead of competing for one field. [Weave](https://rawthoughts.in/weave/) is designed to carry that commercial world together with the work it creates, for people and agents alike.

<a id="continuity-and-mandate-answer-different-questions"></a>

## Continuity and mandate answer different questions

The account manager can preserve the relationship's history: who matters, which concerns recur and what has previously been agreed. An account executive can handle a defined commercial mandate. The two live opportunities still need their own responsibilities and permissions.

| Relationship in the example | Responsibility | Interval or boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Account continuity | Maintain history and coordinate the customer's recurring concerns | Across both opportunities and later handovers |
| New-service opportunity | Qualify the proposed work and carry its agreed next step | This opportunity, under its mandate |
| Renewal opportunity | Resolve the existing service's renewal question | This renewal, not every future sale |
| Delivery authority | Accept a commitment the team can fulfil | The particular scope and terms approved |

One person may hold several of these roles. The record still needs to say which relationship they are acting on. A change in the renewal contact should not rewrite who qualified the new service, and an account-wide conversation should not quietly commit the delivery team. Weave's intended commercial model gives accounts, people, opportunities, mandates and dated relationships separate objects so those changes remain intelligible.

<a id="finding-a-fit-that-can-become-work"></a>

## Finding a fit that can become work

Consider the same account over three reviews. During discovery, the new-service sponsor describes recurring delays and the operating owner offers access to the relevant records. The opportunity has a plausible problem and a way to investigate it. At the next review, the proposed access is unavailable, so the assessment becomes blocked even though the sponsor remains interested. Later, a narrower dataset becomes available and a revised scope can be assessed. The original interest did not disappear; the means of delivering the proposed work changed.

Weave's commercial design keeps those assessments with the evidence, reviewer and policy version that produced each one. The current queue shows the narrower next step and who is pursuing it, while the earlier assessments explain why the team did not proceed sooner. Renewal work at that account continues throughout. Its mandate, sponsor and evidence are different, even if the account manager is the same person.

![Across three reviews, renewal continues while new-service access is offered, becomes unavailable and returns as a narrower dataset, with a workshop still proposed.](https://rawthoughts.in/collections/practice/reading-the-relationship/opportunity-tracks.svg)

The positions show review order, not elapsed time; the workshop remains a proposal, and the renewal follows its own evidence.

That history is useful commercially. It distinguishes a lost opportunity from an access problem, a delayed response from a rejected proposal, and a changed scope from an unexplained drop in score. Buyer, sponsor, technical owner and operating owner become relationships to investigate and maintain, not four extra contact fields whose names imply responsibility.

A promising opportunity is not necessarily an eligible one. Suppose a customer wants a service that the team could technically deliver, but the requested use violates an established boundary. A high opportunity score cannot cancel that disqualifier.

Even for eligible work, a reviewer needs the evidence for and against the fit, including what has gone stale and what is still unknown. Without it, a score can make an unsettled judgment look like measured truth. Weave keeps the policy and evidence behind each assessment as they stood at the time. If a later conversation changes the judgment, the new conclusion supersedes the old one without deleting it. The team can then distinguish a poor original decision from a reasonable decision made before the situation changed.

<a id="from-a-suggested-step-to-an-accepted-obligation"></a>

## From a suggested step to an accepted obligation

An analysis might recommend scheduling a workshop. Before that becomes a commitment, the team and customer need to agree its terms, with someone authorised to commit the team. Until then, it remains a proposed next action with reasons. Moving a card across a board cannot make that agreement for them.

A small team with clear standing authority may need only a concise record of the agreement. The authority is already there, so the process can be light. Silence alone cannot establish it.

<a id="returning-to-the-account"></a>

## Returning to the account

The next account review can now begin somewhere definite. The renewal is proceeding under its existing mandate. The new service has a narrower dataset and a proposed workshop, with a named person still needed to accept that work. The account manager can carry that history into the conversation without promising delivery on either opportunity's behalf.

The two teams also need different parts of the history. The renewal team may need the customer's recurring concern but not the new-service team's restricted technical records. A useful shared picture connects those references while showing where a team has to ask for access. Weave's intended model would make that position available to people and to [Pale](https://rawthoughts.in/pale/): what changed, which commitment remains and who can take the next step. One account can then have continuity without forcing every opportunity, promise and private conversation into one owner field.
