Title: Making a working world intelligible

[Read HTML](https://rawthoughts.in/collections/systems/making-a-working-world-intelligible/)

[Pale](https://rawthoughts.in/pale/) is the meta harness, a harness over the coding tools people already use, so that each asks one description of your world what it may do; this journey asks whether that earns its place.

Suppose you return to an equipment purchase after a week away. The comparison was accepted, the budget changed and part of the order arrived. The person waiting for it still cannot do their work. Before you can help, you have to recover how those facts fit together. An agent returning to the same task faces much of the same work. How much of that understanding could survive the interruption?

That question sits behind [Pale](https://rawthoughts.in/pale/). A description of the working world could connect the request, the approval and the supplier’s record without taking their place. It could also preserve who owes the next response. But somebody has to establish those relationships and keep them useful. Starting with the actual responsibility helps decide which structure is worth adding: a recurring review may need a continuing role, while a one-off comparison may need only someone to prepare it and someone to accept it.

The resulting system should be able to improve too. A better procedure or model might make the next comparison easier, provided we can tell what changed and whether it helped. Eventually the question returns to the people carrying the work. Does the proposed machinery save them enough reconstruction and confusion to repay its preparation and upkeep? Their existing tools may already do the job well. Understanding the architecture lets us ask what it contributes, including when another layer would give them more to carry.
