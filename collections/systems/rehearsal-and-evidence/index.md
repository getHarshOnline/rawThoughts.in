Title: Rehearsal and evidence

[Read HTML](https://rawthoughts.in/collections/systems/rehearsal-and-evidence/)

Rehearsing a decision is how a direction can be tried before anyone decides, and it can expose what an announcement leaves out: the manager who has to explain it, the quiet reader who leaves, or the group carrying a cost hidden by the average. An imagined objection can make the proposal more interesting. A [library's appointment system](https://rawthoughts.in/collections/systems/rehearsal-and-evidence/before-asking-an-imagined-audience/) promises predictable staffing, yet a visitor with an unpredictable schedule could lose access. The useful question is no longer “will people like it?” It is whether the library can preserve both a reliable booked slot and an unplanned visit. Rehearsal has found a tension the decision-maker can investigate.

The value of that discovery depends on how the objection arose. Did the scenario omit walk-in capacity? Did [the interaction rules](https://rawthoughts.in/collections/systems/rehearsal-and-evidence/how-opinions-move/) make every generated visitor unusually pliable, or isolate a concern from anyone able to answer it? And whose response would never appear in a conversation, because [they quietly left or never adopted the change](https://rawthoughts.in/collections/systems/rehearsal-and-evidence/the-audience-that-does-not-reply/)? These questions keep a vivid rehearsal attached to the actual choice. The proposed design of [RawFish](https://rawthoughts.in/rawfish/) centres on that return: a more precise alternative, a neglected constraint and an observation worth seeking from the people affected.
