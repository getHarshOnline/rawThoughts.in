Weave

In Weave’s design, a customer’s failed export stays linked to its investigation, review and the check that it works again. Customer relationships and work tracking share the same records, so people and agents can follow the work from the chat rooms, assistants and browser clients they already use.

One world for relationships and work

Those records are the reason Weave exists. Whoever comes next, whether a person, an agent or both, picks up the story from what was kept rather than from a long conversation. That matters most for agents, which decide far more often than people: by the time one writes, what it read has usually changed, so the story it picks up has to be the current one. A customer relationship can include several agreements and many jobs, and Weave lets people and agents return to the original request, the work they agreed to do and the decisions made along the way. Each organisation keeps its own records. Something new enters through Radar, the part of Weave that brings in an observation or a proposed direction for review; it becomes work only when someone takes responsibility for it.

Weave is the part of the products that keeps the records, and it also stands on its own: alone it is a headless Jira and Salesforce, a database of the organisation's records behind a capable interface. No Weave service runs yet; the design is written and the first build, one account taken through to accepted work, is the work in front.

As in Radio became Spotify, the need persists while the way of meeting it changes: agreeing work and seeing it through can now happen in a conversation, while Weave keeps the responsibility, result and acceptance clear for whoever returns to it next.

Let the commitment outlast the conversation

A customer’s export fails. A colleague can investigate the timeout while another improves the error message. Weave keeps both jobs linked to the customer's request, with a clear record of who took each one on and who can accept the result.

  1. Agree the work

    Link the investigation to the customer’s request and the relevant service agreement. Record what result the team agrees to deliver. A possible next step stays a proposal until someone turns it into a commitment.

  2. Accept responsibility

    Record who agreed to supply the trace and explanation. A person or an agent can take on the investigation, with their own identity and role. An agent joins the team follows that responsibility.

  3. Supply the result

    Connect the delivered result to the assignment so it can be reviewed. Handovers preserve earlier contributions, and recurring duties keep a separate occurrence each time, giving a returning colleague the right history.

  4. Review and accept

    The person named to review the investigation decides whether the trace and explanation answer the question. Record what they accept, what needs a correction and who still owes a result.

Questions about Weave

Can an acceptance message close the work?

Yes. Weave keeps acceptance with the person allowed to give it, so the message needs to identify the work and come from that person. If the result meets the agreed conditions, the reviewer only needs to confirm once.

Does Weave depend on Pale?

Weave keeps the records and stands on its own. When both are present, Weave holds the organisational records while Pale coordinates execution and its evidence, and together they let people and agents work inside one organisation and across several, each instance kept separate.

Can separate organisations work together?

Yes. Weave is built for work across organisations, and each keeps its own instance, records and decisions. A client can offer work and a provider can take it on, with each side deciding what to accept and what the other may see. Weave also serves teams that work without agents.

Is there an app?

Weave has no app of its own; it meets people where they already work: it describes each card and view, and the surface a team already uses renders it: a chat room, an assistant or a browser client built on the public interfaces. A card shows the current record, says when it is stale and leaves out what the reader may not see, wherever it appears.

Where should I start?

Start with the Weave guide, since Weave is for keeping a commitment clear from request to acceptance. It follows one failed export from the customer's message into two jobs, and shows who takes each one on and who accepts the result.
